Account types

How the same money could grow in different accounts

This hypothetical illustration puts the same money into three kinds of account and shows how each one could look after taxes. Change the rate, the monthly amounts and the tax assumptions to see how the picture might move. It is a teaching tool, not a forecast.

The three kinds of account

Taxable brokerage

A regular investment account

No special tax treatment. Tax may be owed each year on dividends, and again on gains when investments are sold. There is no contribution limit.

Tax-deferred

Like a traditional 401(k) or IRA

Money may go in before tax and grow without yearly tax. Withdrawals are taxed as income.

Tax-exempt

Like a Roth 401(k) or Roth IRA

Money goes in after tax. Growth and qualified withdrawals may be tax-free.

How this is calculated
  • Money grows at the yearly rate divided by 12 each month. Each deposit lands at the end of the month, and every account starts at $0.
  • Tax-exempt: the monthly amount goes in after tax. In this illustration its growth and its withdrawals are not taxed.
  • Tax-deferred: with "Same take-home cost", each deposit is the monthly amount divided by one minus your tax rate today, as if it were made before tax. With "Same amount in", the deposit is the monthly amount. The balance at the end is reduced by the tax rate on withdrawals.
  • Taxable: each month, the share of growth taxed each year (think dividends) is taxed at the rate on investment gains and the rest of the growth stays invested. The gain that has not been taxed yet is taxed at that rate as if the account were sold at the end.
  • "What you could keep after taxes" is the balance minus the tax owed when taken out. "Added by the additional contribution" is that figure with the additional amount minus the same figure with the current monthly investment alone.

Hypothetical illustration for education only, not tax, legal or investment advice. The same return before tax is assumed in all three accounts, and the figures are in future (nominal) dollars, not adjusted for inflation. Contribution limits, income limits for Roth accounts, early-withdrawal penalties, required distributions, state taxes and changes in tax law are not modeled. Returns vary and are not guaranteed.
