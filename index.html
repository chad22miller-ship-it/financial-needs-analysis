Section 1 · The basics

What is an FI number?

FI stands for financial independence. An FI number is the amount of money that, if it were invested, might be able to pay for the life you have in mind without needing a paycheck.

A common rule of thumb suggests that taking about 4% a year out of an invested nest egg could last. Under that assumption the math is simple: take a year of spending and divide it by 4%.

For example, spending $2,000 a month would be $24,000 a year. Divided by 4%, that illustrates an FI number of $600,000. The calculator below also raises spending for inflation to the retirement year, so an illustrated number could be higher.

The 4% rule is a rule of thumb, not a promise. Actual returns vary and may be lower.

How this is calculated
  • Money is assumed to grow at the yearly rate you pick, compounded monthly, with contributions added at the end of each month.
  • The monthly spending is in today's dollars. In this illustration it is raised 3% a year (the Assumed inflation slider) to the retirement year, so it can be compared with the illustrated savings.
  • An FI number is illustrated as that future spending for a year divided by 4%, a common withdrawal rule of thumb.
  • Assumed Social Security and pension income would reduce the amount savings might need to cover.
  • Taxes, fees and market swings are not included. Actual returns vary and are never guaranteed.

Where a Financial Needs Analysis comes in

Yearly spending is often the hard part to pin down. A Financial Needs Analysis looks at the whole picture together. From there it could illustrate an FI number and a monthly amount that might help reach it.

  1. 1Your budgetWhat you spend each month now, and what you might want to spend later.
  2. 2Your retirementWhen you might stop working, and which income, like Social Security or a pension, could help cover it.
  3. 3Your time and savingsHow many years there could be, what you have invested so far, and what you might add each month.
  4. 4An FI numberA number you could aim for, and a monthly amount that might help reach it.
